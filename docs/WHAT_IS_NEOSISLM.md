# What Is NeosisLM?

## The Problem

Every researcher knows this cycle: you read fifty papers, you brainstorm wild new ideas, you connect dots nobody has connected before — and then someone asks, "Can you cite that?" and the magic stops. You spend hours retracing your steps, hunting for the exact paragraph in the exact PDF that backs the brilliant claim you just made.

The thinking brain and the citing brain don't work in the same mode. Brainstorming demands speculation, leaps of logic, exploration. Citation demands precision, provenance, and strict grounding. Every existing tool forces you to choose one or the other.

NotebookLM grounds you in your sources — but it can't autonomously chase new leads. GPT Deep Research explores the open web — but it can't guarantee that anything it says actually came from your uploaded data. You end up switching between tools, copy-pasting, and hoping your citations survived the journey.

## The Solution

NeosisLM doesn't make you choose. It gives you two distinct cognitive modes inside a single, shared workspace:

### 🧠 Research Mode — Your Brainstorming Brain
Give NeosisLM a broad objective: *"Map the current landscape of mechanistic interpretability in large language models."* It wakes up a fleet of autonomous agents that plan a research strategy, scrape the latest publications and articles from the web, synthesize findings, detect contradictions, and map the entire conceptual space into a visual **Knowledge Graph** — all without you lifting a finger.

This is the mode for exploration. For discovering things you didn't even know to look for.

### 📎 Ground Mode — Your Citation Brain
Now switch to Ground Mode. Ask: *"What does the Smith et al. 2024 paper specifically argue about superposition in transformer attention heads?"* NeosisLM answers **strictly** from the documents in your workspace. No hallucinations. No internet. Every claim links directly to the exact source paragraph. If the answer isn't in your files, it tells you honestly rather than making something up.

This is the mode for writing. For defending your thesis. For passing peer review.

### The Magic: They Share a Brain
The critical insight is that both modes operate on the **same memory fabric**. When Research Mode discovers a groundbreaking new finding from the web, that finding gets stored permanently in your workspace's knowledge base. The next time you're in Ground Mode writing your literature review, that finding is right there — citable, traceable, grounded.

Your brainstorming feeds your citations. Your citations inform your next brainstorm. It's one continuous research loop instead of two disconnected tools.

## Who Is This For?

- **PhD candidates** drowning in literature reviews who need both breadth (discovery) and depth (citation).
- **Research labs** that want a shared knowledge base where every team member's findings are instantly available and traceable.
- **Independent researchers** and science writers who need to rapidly explore unfamiliar fields while maintaining rigorous sourcing standards.
- **Interdisciplinary teams** working across domains who need to discover unexpected connections between fields — and then verify them.
